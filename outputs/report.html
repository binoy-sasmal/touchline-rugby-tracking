Diagnostic evidence, not a benchmark. Annotations were drawn by Codex through visual inspection before inference and have not been independently human reviewed. Eight sparse frames from one short touch-rugby shot cannot establish whole-match accuracy or robust identity tracking.

What runs

YOLOX-S ONNX person detections → two-pass confidence association with constant-velocity box prediction → observed track labels and JSONL export. The tracker is a small baseline, without appearance embeddings, camera compensation or player names.

Where it fails

Motion blur and overlap can hide people or merge boxes. Camera panning changes apparent velocity. A high-confidence threshold can delay a new track; low-confidence observations may recover existing tracks but cannot create one. The labels use visible body extent, while the detector may infer a fuller body, which can lower IoU. IDs have meaning only within this continuous shot.

Evaluation counts people whose box centre is inside x=30..610, y=30..320 and whose visible box height is at least 40 px. Includes officials. Smaller/distant and edge-centred people are outside this declared scope. Detection metrics use raw detector outputs; identity switches use tracked matches only.

Timing boundaries

60 consecutive frames, 25 fps source. Model load 103 ms; first-frame warm-up 207 ms. Median tracker 0.28 ms. Measured loop throughput 6.4 fps includes decode, detector, tracker, rendering and intermediate encoding; excludes model load, warm-up and the separate final H.264 export (0.33 s). This is not a real-time or GPU claim.
